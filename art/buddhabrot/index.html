How it works

The Mandelbrot set asks, for each point c of the complex plane, whether the orbit

z₀ = 0
zₙ₊₁ = zₙ² + c

stays bounded. The usual picture colours each c by how quickly its orbit escapes. The Buddhabrot looks at the orbits themselves: every c that escapes leaves a trail of points z₁, z₂, …, and each of those adds one to a counter at its pixel. Millions of trails pile up into the seated figure that gives the image its name. The real axis runs from top to bottom here, so the figure sits upright.

Most random points escape after a few steps and only add noise near the centre. The interesting orbits are long, and they start right at the edge of the set. So the explorer does not sample the plane at random. It first renders a coarse Mandelbrot grid, collects every pair of neighbouring cells where one is inside and one outside, and then moves each pair closer to the boundary: a random point near the middle of the pair replaces whichever end is on the same side. After a few dozen of these steps the outside point sits a hair's breadth from the set, where orbits take the longest to escape.

Optional nearby points scatter a Gaussian cloud around each boundary sample; some of them land inside the set and add their full, never escaping orbits, which fills in the body. Everything after the histogram, from scaling and clipping to blur and colour, runs instantly in your browser without recomputing the orbits. The heavy part is spread over Web Workers, one per CPU core.

Zooming in breaks this. The orbits that cross a small window mostly start far away from it, and only a tiny fraction of all orbits cross it at all. The Metropolis method samples where it matters: it runs many random walks over the starting points c, each one trying a small jump (or, rarely, a jump anywhere) and keeping it with a probability that depends on how many of the new orbit's points land in the view. Walks therefore linger on the orbits that draw the window. Each orbit is weighted by one over its number of hits in the view, which undoes that preference, so the result is the plain Buddhabrot of the window, only with far less noise. The walks start from boundary points of the whole set whose orbits reach the view.
