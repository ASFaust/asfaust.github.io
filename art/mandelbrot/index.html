Minimal Mandelbrot

Fractals are usually drawn in rainbow bands. These pictures use a single hue on white, with the detail coming from the border of the set alone.

Where to look

A point c of the plane belongs to the Mandelbrot set if z ← z² + c, started at z = c, stays bounded. The interesting pictures lie along its border, so “New place” searches for a place there. It starts at a random point outside the set and keeps proposing nearby points, moving whenever one takes longer to escape. Points that escape slowly lie close to the set. The proposals shrink with the estimated distance to the set, at a rate that is itself random, so the walk ends right on the border after a thousand steps. The zoom is then drawn evenly on a logarithmic scale, between a view 0.002 wide and one about four times the distance to the set. The seed fixes all of these choices.

Two measurements

For every sample the iteration gives two numbers. The escape time is the number of steps until |z| passes 20. The distance estimate is 2 |z| log |z| / |dz/dc|, where the derivative follows the iteration as dz ← 2 z dz + 1. It says how far the sample is from the set, and here it is measured in samples.

The grey value starts as the escape time, rescaled so that the fastest escape is white and the inside of the set is black. Within one sample of the border it is lowered to the distance estimate itself. The thin filaments that escape-time banding hides become dark lines, while the open areas stay light, and never darker than 0.2. “Border width” stretches that band.

One colour

The grey value is rescaled to the range 0 to 1 and runs along a ramp in OKLCh, the polar form of Björn Ottosson’s OKLab colour space. Its lightness and chroma move in a straight line from their values at the set to their values far out, at one fixed hue. The ramp covers only the points outside the set; the inside is black, or white with “Invert”. In OKLab equal steps look equal, and unlike in the older CIELAB a hue keeps its colour as it gets lighter or darker. Where a colour lies outside what the screen can show, it gives up chroma and keeps its lightness and hue.

Each pixel averages the grey values of its samples outside the set before looking up the colour, and mixes the result, in OKLab, with the colour of the inside for the share of samples that fall inside the set. Because lightness and chroma are linear in the grey value, this is the same as averaging the colours in OKLab, so thin filaments fade evenly. Unless “Circle” is off, the image is cut to a disk.

The page computes in plain double precision, spread over several Web Workers. The samples stay in the workers, so a new colour needs no new iterations. Below a radius of about 10−11 the numbers run out of digits and the picture turns blocky.

Origin

This is a port of Mandelbounty, an earlier Python and C++ generator. Its search and distance estimate carry over unchanged, and so does the grey value. The colouring has moved from HSV to OKLCh, and the random numbers come from a different generator, so its seeds give different pictures here.
