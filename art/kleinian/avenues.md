# Beyond Grandma's Recipe: New Avenues for Limit-Set Fractals

## What makes the Möbius pipeline work

Grandma's recipe is productive because it chains five ingredients:

1. **Finite parameters.** Three traces determine a two-generator group up to conjugacy (Fricke).
2. **Explicit construction.** Closed-form matrices from chosen parameters.
3. **Discreteness.** The group acts properly discontinuously, so the limit set is a genuine fractal, not a dense smear.
4. **Contraction along words.** The Cayley tree can be searched depth-first with an ε-cutoff.
5. **Conformality.** Small pieces look like undistorted copies of large ones.

Each avenue below keeps some of these and breaks others. The interesting question is always: *which ingredient survives, and what replaces the broken one?*

| Avenue | Params | Discreteness test | Conformal | Rendering difficulty | Novelty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reflection groups / Apollonian | discrete (Coxeter data) + continuous | Coxeter / Vinberg | yes | low | low |
| 3D Möbius / Vahlen matrices | many | hard | yes | medium | medium |
| Pseudo-Kleinian (inversion + fold) | few | none (heuristic) | no | low (DE ray-marching) | medium |
| Riley / Maskit slices | ℂ (1 complex) | Jørgensen, pleating rays | – | medium | low–medium |
| Convex projective, PSL(3,ℝ) | Fock–Goncharov coords | Anosov / positivity | no | medium | **high** |
| Complex hyperbolic, PU(2,1) | triangle group angles + 1 real | partially open | no | high | **high** |
| Character variety dynamics | k ∈ ℂ | n/a (dynamics) | – | medium | high |
| Matings / Schwarz reflections | few | via combinatorics | yes | high | high |
| Non-conformal IFS | many | n/a | no | low | low |
| p-adic Schottky groups | few | Schottky condition | n/a | needs invented visual language | high |

## 1. Reflection groups and circle packings

**Idea.** Replace Möbius generators by inversions in circles. Kleinian reflection groups produce Apollonian gaskets, Soddy packings, and more general "crystallographic packings" (Kontorovich–Nakamura).

**Why interesting.** Coxeter diagrams give a discrete, combinatorial design space. Each diagram, together with continuous deformations, gives a family of packings. Integral packings connect to number theory.

**How to render.** Use the same DFS over reduced words in reflections, or draw circles directly. This is cheap and exact.

**Open threads.**
- Enumerate Coxeter diagrams systematically and render the whole catalogue.
- Deform toward the boundary of the discrete locus and watch the packing degenerate.

## 2. Higher-dimensional Möbius groups

**Idea.** Möb(S³) via Clifford-algebra Vahlen matrices, or simply sphere inversions in ℝ³. This gives 3D Schottky groups, Apollonian sphere packings, and quasi-Fuchsian surfaces made of spheres.

**Why interesting.** Conformality survives, so you get genuinely self-similar 3D structure.

**What breaks.** There is no tidy trace parametrization: Vahlen-matrix traces don't determine conjugacy classes as cleanly.

**How to render.** Use ray-marching with distance estimators, or sphere-tree rendering (Jos Leys' 3D Kleinian work).

**Open threads.**
- A "3D Grandma's recipe": a minimal family with explicit parametrization that reliably produces discrete groups.

## 3. Pseudo-Kleinian fractals (sphere inversion + folds)

**Idea.** Knighty's construction interleaves sphere inversion with box/plane folds. The result is not a group action in the strict sense, but it inherits Kleinian look-and-feel.

**Why interesting.** It is cheap to explore and visually rich, with lots of architectural structure.

**What breaks.** There is no discreteness theory and no limit-set theory. It is purely heuristic.

**Open threads.**
- Characterise when the escape-time structure actually matches a Kleinian limit set.
- Find a principled family between true Kleinian groups and Mandelbox-type maps.

## 4. Parameter space: Riley slice, Maskit slice, and friends

**Idea.** Render the *set of parameters* giving discrete groups, rather than a single limit set.
- **Maskit slice:** one complex parameter, with tr a = 2 and tr b = μ.
- **Riley slice:** groups generated by two parabolics.

Their boundaries are fractal, a Kleinian analogue of the Mandelbrot set.

**Why interesting.** These pictures are less rendered than limit sets. There is also structure to exploit: pleating rays (Keen–Series) give coordinates for the slices, and cusp groups sit densely on the boundary.

**How to render.**
- Escape-time-style tests, e.g. word-length bounds plus Jørgensen inequality checks.
- Alternatively, trace pleating rays and locate cusps by solving trace polynomials.

**Open threads.**
- 2D slices through the full ℂ² parameter space, beyond the classical ones.
- Animate the limit set while moving along a pleating ray toward a cusp.

## 5. Convex projective structures: PSL(3,ℝ) ★

**Idea.** These are representations of surface groups into PSL(3,ℝ) acting on ℝP², in the Hitchin component and more generally Anosov representations. Each one preserves a strictly convex domain Ω, and its limit set is ∂Ω, a C¹ (not C²) curve. Benoist calls these domains "divisible convex sets".

**Why interesting.** It is the projective analogue of quasi-Fuchsian space: finite-dimensional, explicitly parametrized, and the subject of active research. The images also look unlike Möbius fractals. You get convex curves with fractal *regularity* rather than fractal shape, tilings of Ω by projective polygons, and Hilbert-metric geometry.

**Parameters.** Use Goldman's coordinates or Fock–Goncharov coordinates (positive configurations of flags). Positivity of the coordinates guarantees discreteness and faithfulness. This is the analogue of Grandma's "pick traces, get a group".

**How to render.**
- DFS over words, plotting attracting fixed points in ℝP².
- Tile Ω by images of a fundamental domain.
- Colour by the Hilbert metric.

**Open threads.**
- A "PSL(3,ℝ) Grandma's recipe": explicit matrices from Fock–Goncharov coordinates for the punctured torus or pair of pants.
- Degenerations: drive coordinates to 0 or ∞ and watch Ω collapse toward a triangle or polygon.
- Generalise to PSL(n,ℝ), where the limit curve lives in ℝP^{n−1}, and render projections.

## 6. Complex hyperbolic triangle groups: PU(2,1) ★

**Idea.** These are groups generated by complex reflections acting on complex hyperbolic 2-space. Their limit sets live in S³ = Heisenberg group ∪ {∞}.

**Why interesting.** The geometry is genuinely non-conformal: the Heisenberg group has a sub-Riemannian, CR structure. Limit sets can be wild, and discreteness is only partially understood. Schwartz's conjectures on triangle groups were open for a long time. Few people have rendered these.

**Parameters.** Triangle group angles (p,q,r) plus one real angular invariant.

**How to render.**
- Map S³ to the Heisenberg group, then project or slice to 3D, or use stereographic projection.
- Plot fixed points of loxodromic words.

**Open threads.**
- Rendering conventions themselves (projections, slicing, colouring by Cygan metric) are underdeveloped.
- Empirically map the discreteness locus in the (p,q,r, angular invariant) space.

## 7. Dynamics on the character variety

**Idea.** The trace equation from Grandma's recipe,

  x² + y² + z² − xyz = k,

is itself acted on by the mapping class group via Markov-type moves such as (x,y,z) ↦ (x, y, xy − z). Treat this as a *dynamical system* on the cubic surface.

**Why interesting.**
- For real k, the action has regions of chaos and of proper discontinuity (Goldman).
- For complex k, it gives Julia-set-like invariant sets (Cantat–Loray).

This is the fractal structure of the space of all Grandma groups, seen dynamically.

**How to render.**
- Take escape-time plots on real or complex slices of the surface, iterating random or fixed words of the Markov moves.

**Open threads.**
- Connect the escape locus back to which limit sets the points produce, and render both side by side.

## 8. Matings: Kleinian groups × rational maps

**Idea.** Sullivan's dictionary is made literal. Correspondences and Schwarz reflections in quadrature domains produce dynamical systems that behave like a Kleinian group on one part of the sphere and like a polynomial on another.

**Why interesting.** These are hybrid fractals, combining a Julia set of a polynomial with the limit set of a Kleinian (often Hecke) group. Very recent and active work (Bullett–Lomonaco; Lee–Lyubich–Makarov–Mukherjee).

**Parameters.** There are few, and they have connectedness loci analogous to the Mandelbrot set.

**How to render.** Use inverse iteration of the correspondence or the Schwarz reflection, then colour by which regime a point belongs to.

**Open threads.**
- Visual catalogue of parameter slices.
- Generalise the reflection-group side using avenue 1.

## 9. Non-conformal IFS

**Idea.** Drop the group and iterate contractions directly.
- **Conformal IFS** (Mauldin–Urbański) keep self-similarity.
- **Affine IFS** give self-affine sets: Sierpiński variants, Bedford–McMullen carpets, Barnsley ferns.
- **Projective IFS** are a natural bridge to avenue 5.

**Why interesting.** Projective IFS are relatively unexplored. They also connect to Barnsley–Vince attractors and to limit sets of Anosov representations of free semigroups.

**How to render.** Use the chaos game or deterministic DFS.

## 10. Non-archimedean Kleinian groups

**Idea.** Schottky groups in PGL(2,ℚ_p) act on the Bruhat–Tits tree. Their limit sets live in ℙ¹(ℚ_p), or in the Berkovich projective line.

**Why interesting.** These carry deep connections to Mumford curves and tropical geometry.

**What's missing.** A natural visual language. Rendering requires *inventing* an embedding: trees drawn in the hyperbolic plane, or ℤ_p mapped via digit expansions.

**Open threads.**
- Pick an embedding of ℚ_p into the plane and see whether Grandma-style families produce recognisable structure.

## Cross-cutting project ideas

- **Unified renderer.** Build an engine abstracting "group = generators as matrices in G acting on space X". Plug in PSL(2,ℂ)/ℂ̂, PSL(3,ℝ)/ℝP², and PU(2,1)/Heisenberg, and reuse the DFS, ε-pruning, and fixed-point plotting across all of them.
- **Search over parameter spaces.** Discreteness loci are thin, fractal sets in parameter space. Novelty search or MAP-Elites over Fock–Goncharov or triangle-group parameters could fill the space. Behaviour descriptors could be limit-set dimension estimates, curve regularity, or tiling statistics, giving a quality-diversity atlas of a new geometry.
- **Degeneration movies.** Each avenue has a boundary where groups stop being discrete. Animating limit sets as parameters approach the boundary (cusps, collapses, wild limits) is where the most striking images tend to be.
- **Dimension estimation.** Compute Hausdorff or critical-exponent estimates along the way. This adds a scalar to colour parameter space by, and checks whether the renderer is faithful.
