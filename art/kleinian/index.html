How it works

A Möbius map sends a point z of the plane to (pz + q) / (rz + s). It moves circles to circles, and it is determined up to scale by the matrix [p q; r s]. Take two such maps a and b, together with their inverses A and B, and compose them in every order: a, ab, abA, abAb, … The points that these words pile up on, however long they get, form the limit set of the group.

Most choices of maps give a chaotic mess. The beautiful ones are those where the commutator abAB is parabolic, a map with a single fixed point. Then the plane folds into two punctured tori glued at their cusps, the group is quasi-Fuchsian, and the limit set is a single closed fractal curve. Up to a change of coordinates such a pair of maps is fixed by two complex numbers, the traces p + s of a and b. “Grandma’s recipe” from Indra’s Pearls turns the two traces into matrices.

Drawing the curve

Every point of the limit set has an address, an infinite word in a, b, A, B in which no letter is followed by its inverse. The search walks the tree of these words depth first, always turning the same way, so it travels once around the curve in order. Below a word W it compares two points: W applied to the fixed points of the two commutators that can follow its last letter. These are the two ends of the piece of curve under W. Once they are less than ε apart it draws a line between them and backs up. Otherwise it goes one letter deeper.

Where the curve passes through a parabolic point the words converge slowly, so the depth is capped. With “follow narrow necks” the search also checks the fixed point of the last letter itself, the tip of the whorl, so it does not cut across a frond attached by a thin neck. For a zoomed view, a first coarse pass measures which part of the plane each pair of opening letters fills. Any branch that maps its part completely outside the view is skipped, so a deep zoom costs about as much as the full picture. The work is split over four Web Workers, one for each first letter.

The Maskit slice

Fix the trace of b at 2, so b is parabolic, and vary the trace of a. The values that give a discrete group form the Maskit slice, the shaded region of the first trace plane. Its edge is fractal, and on it sit the cusp groups: for each fraction p/q a particular word W(p/q) becomes parabolic too, and the limit set pinches into chains of tangent circles with period q. The trace of W(p/q) is a polynomial of degree q in the trace of a. The explorer builds it along the Farey tree with the identity tr uv + tr uV = tr u · tr v, then finds the right root with Newton’s method. Fractions converging to an irrational number lead to degenerate groups, whose limit sets fill whole regions.

For other values of tr b no such formula is known, so the trace planes test each point numerically. They run the same search, coarsely and without drawing, and measure distances on the Riemann sphere rather than in the plane, so the size and position of the limit set do not matter. Each point is searched twice, the second time with half the tolerance. For a discrete group that multiplies the work by about 2D, where D ≤ 2 is the dimension of its limit set. A group that is not discrete may look tidy at the coarse scale, but its words stop contracting below it, and the second search runs out of its budget. The number of steps rises towards the edge of the discrete region, which gives the shading. It is a numerical test, not a proof, but with tr b = 2 its edge lies on the exact curve of cusps.

With the commutator trace set to 0 instead of −2, (abAB)² is the identity, the group is no longer free, and many words name the same map. A small finite-state automaton from Indra’s Pearls then tells the search which words to skip.
