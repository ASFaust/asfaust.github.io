How it works

Each pixel is given in polar coordinates: its distance r from the centre and its angle θ. An inner wave modulates the phase of an outer one:

inner = sin(d1·r + a1·θ + t1·time)
value = sin(π·warp·inner + a2·θ + d2·c(r) + t2·time)
ink where (value + 1) / 2 ≥ c(r)
c(r)  = 3·(r − 0.5)² + 0.5

The whole-number angle factors decide the rotational symmetry: three petals, five, or none at all, which leaves only rings. Mixing radius and angle turns petals into spirals, and the warp factor bends clean petals into ornament.

The threshold c(r) does most of the work. It is lowest halfway out and climbs above 1 near the centre and the rim, where no wave can reach it. So every shape lives inside a ring, and its strokes taper as they leave it, like ink running out of a brush.
